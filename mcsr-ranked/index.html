Correct tests ≠ live accuracy

“100/100” means the code recovered the known fixture answers. People and real clients have not been tested end to end.

Probability ≠ certainty

“79.08%” is a uniform-seed reference. About 20.92% of that favourable group still has zero eyes.

A guarantee has conditions

“At least one” follows from the code only when the observations, version and generation settings are correct.

01
Exact-count prototype

Read where an item stops.

A falling gravel block breaks on a floor button or torch and becomes a raw item at a known location. Its sideways travel reveals its randomized starting velocity.

OBSERVE

6 drops + 3 drops

Use two fresh spawn locations. Measure each settled item, collect it, then release the next. One gravel block is reusable.

RECONSTRUCT

Position → velocity

Triangulate the same white hitbox edge using two F3 yaw readings. Work backward through the motion and RNG.

CALCULATE

Seed → eye count

Keep the seeds that fit every observation. Compute the twelve frame checks; report agreement or ambiguity.

100/100 correctoffline fixtures0.138–0.422 s per solve

Actual observation cost: 9 drops mean 18 angle readings, two known viewing positions, and a controlled landing area. Setup and human recording time were not measured. A tenth, unused drop is recommended as a check.

The calculation, step by step
  1. Each sight line is playerXZ + t × (−sin(yaw), cos(yaw)). Convert yaw to radians and intersect the two lines. Aim at the same northwest vertical F3+B hitbox edge, not the rotating item artwork. Add (0.125, 0.125) to get the centre.
  2. Subtract the breaker block’s integer X/Z. The item starts at relative (0.5, 0.5). On the tested normal-friction floor:
    x ≈ 0.5 + 9.526808774 × vx
vx ≈ (x − 0.5) / 9.526808774
RNG fraction u ≈ (vx + 0.1) / 0.2
    The same calculation applies to Z. Relative X=0.26, for example, implies vx≈−0.0252 and u≈0.374.
  3. The multiplier is a geometric sum of motion: C = (1+a+…+a⁷) + a⁸/(1−g), with a=0.98f and g=0.6f×0.98f using Minecraft’s float rounding. The real movement cutoff makes the final position slightly different from this infinite-decay limit.
  4. Fit the Java generator to the twelve X/Z components from six items. Each constructor consumes an unused yaw float and two doubles: five generator steps per item. The solver uses bounded intervals, not exact decimal equality.
  5. A first-site mixed state leaves 16 missing seed bits. Reverse the mixing function for the 65,536 high-bit completions of each candidate state, then reject full seeds inconsistent with the second site’s three drops. Nine drops are not universally sufficient.

The missing proof is the human readout.

The solver assumes total error no larger than ±0.04 block per axis. In 40,804 synthetic geometry cases, assumed ±0.05° aiming error followed by 0.1° yaw rounding gave at most 0.01958 block axis error after centimetre rounding, with exact station positions. That does not establish human precision or real-client replication accuracy.

Keep an air gap above the breaker; use a dry, unobstructed full-block normal-friction floor. Prior item spawns at the same key, merging, camera movement and different surfaces can invalidate the model. The current lab solver is Overworld-only. No complete live Mixin/client/portal-visit pipeline has been tested.

02
Exact count / candidates

Record gravel. Add another clue.

Flint and non-flint results constrain a deterministic random sequence. Ordered blaze drops and, if needed, dead-bush counts help separate seeds that look identical through gravel alone.

Both variants recorded eligible, unlooted blaze kills until six rods dropped: 7–19 kills, mean 12.20. That is not the same as six blaze kills. More flints reduced computation, but required more mining.

Why the sequence reveals information

An ordinary gravel break supplies an inequality:

flint  → nextFloat() < 0.1f
gravel → nextFloat() ≥ 0.1f

Flints at indices 3 and 5 therefore also tell us that breaks 1, 2 and 4 were non-flints. A lattice-based reverser finds generator states consistent with the complete sequence, then reverses the category initialization.

The other observations reject candidates whose BLAZE nextInt(2) or DEAD_BUSH nextInt(3) outputs disagree. The categories have separate state but derive from the same seed. Two roots can produce the same entire FLINT stream and different eye counts; more gravel alone cannot separate that pair.

One concrete fixture

For seed 8675309, ten flints arrived at breaks:

3, 5, 10, 11, 22, 25, 33, 38, 56, 58

Blaze outcomes 0,1,0,1,0,1,0,1,1,1 left three candidates. First bush count 1 left one, predicting 2 eyes. Twelve flints by break 82 resolved the same answer without bushes.

The older sixteen-flint method passed 20 fixtures representing 18 distinct low-48 seeds, using 110–259 breaks and 0–3 bushes. That is a different cohort, not another twenty independent confirmations of the new comparison.

Conditions: exact order and known stream start; ordinary unenchanted gravel; no Looting, missed eligible kills or uncertain pickups for the blaze model. Auxiliary logs begin at their own stream starts. These are code tests, not a played recording session. Neither flint count guarantees resolution for every seed.

Reference probability

The stronghold’s angle is a clue.

Read the correctly identified first-ring stronghold start chunk from normal triangulation—not the player’s chunk or the portal-room chunk.

No observation71.75%
→
Ideal favourable phase79.08%

P(at least one prefilled eye), under the uniform-seed reference. Not calibrated to Ranked’s private seed filter or a player’s route.

First convert chunk coordinates into block coordinatesX = 16 × chunkX; Z = 16 × chunkZ
phase = atan2(Z, X) in degrees, normalized modulo 120°

For an exact default first-ring start chunk, phase 92°–112° is a conservative favourable shortcut. Phase 8°–76° excludes one particular frame, not all twelve. Extra triangulation uncertainty needs an additional bound.

Shared random bits, not twelve independent coin flips

The original stronghold-ring angle and standardized frame 0 expose overlapping bits of the same initial Java random draw. Frame 0 fills when the initial angle is at least 324.0000128746°.

A first-ring phase leaves three labels, 120° apart. At or above phase 84.0000128746°, one label permits frame 0; below it, none does. Biome relocation can shift the observed angle, so the calculator widens it by asin((169.7056 + extraPositionError) / distance) and leaves unsafe boundaries unresolved.

For intuition only, 1 − (2/3) × 0.9¹¹ ≈ 79.08%. The actual reported probabilities use the full standardized twelve-frame formula, not that independence approximation.

05
Conditional minimum

A rare temple removes the ambiguity.

You need all three observations—not just any east-facing desert temple:

  1. The central blue terracotta block is exactly at X=10, Z=10, identifying an origin-chunk temple.
  2. The main entrance faces outward east.
  3. A correctly identified first-ring stronghold has a safely favourable phase, including the necessary error bound.
Under those conditions and standardized eyesat least 1 prefilled eyeNot the exact total
Why this is a guarantee rather than better odds

At the origin, the temple’s first orientation draw fixes the ring’s initial angle to [270°, 360°). Intersect that quarter with the safely favourable stronghold-phase intervals: every remaining angle is above the frame-0 threshold. Frame 0 must contain an eye.

13,041 conditional code cases matched. This does not mean that many actual origin temples were found—the broad tests supplied a hypothetical quarter anchor. Ten separate position/biome-eligible practice fixtures were checked.

Example fixture −8152824522481496679, stronghold start chunk (151,−43): the method guarantees a minimum of one; the portal code predicts two. There was no subsequent human portal visit.

An east-facing origin temple alone is not enough for certainty. A separate 100-million-seed placement/biome study found 10,504 eligible origin temples; the 2,590 east-facing cases had 80.42% ≥1-eye outcomes. This is still not live Ranked-filter calibration.

Rare, conditional, and version-specific. Misidentifying the centre, entrance, ring or start chunk invalidates the inference. Like method 04, it uses the Overworld generation seed directly and does not need loot-RNG/world-seed equality.

THE COMMON LAST STEP

A seed gives twelve deterministic checks.

“Random” here does not mean unpredictable once the starting state is known. The exact-count methods recover candidates, then calculate what each candidate would put in the portal.

The generator and exact portal formula

The 48-bit internal Java state advances as:

q_next = (25214903917 × q + 11) mod 2⁴⁸
nextFloat = (q_next >>> 24) / 2²⁴

For the Overworld generation seed S, standardized eyes are equivalent to:

r = JavaRandom(S)
a = r.nextLong() | 1
b = r.nextLong() | 1
for i = 0..11:
    p = (i × a + (2 × i) × b) XOR S
    eye[i] = JavaRandom(p).nextFloat() > 0.9f
count = number of true eye[i] values

Use Java 64-bit wrapping arithmetic. | 1 makes each multiplier odd. JavaRandom(s) initializes its state to (s XOR 0x5DEECE66D) & (2⁴⁸−1). The eye count depends only on the seed’s low 48 bits.

The formula was separately compared with the shipped mixin method and original Minecraft RNG code. Those isolated checks do not establish full live injection or portal-generation integration.

Two seeds must not be confused: methods 01/02 recover the configured RNG seed. They can substitute it for S only when it equals the Overworld generation seed. The gravel categories use successive nextLong seeds from JavaRandom(RNGseed + 4262064045) in RNG base version 2; the motion method uses a different position-salted mixing path.

NEGATIVE RESULTS COUNT TOO

Not every clue became a shortcut.

Falling breakers as flint generators
They drop raw gravel without evaluating the flint loot roll. Useful for method 01, not extra FLINT observations.
Ordinary hoppers and comparators
Stack merging and slot extraction lose the original sequence; fullness is not a flint-event log. Custom recording machinery is not ruled out.
Five coarse landing bins per axis
Two reversals timed out at 180 seconds. No fast coarse-bin solver was delivered; a timeout is not an impossibility proof.
Fortune III / Fortune II
Always-flint Fortune III outcomes carry no flint/gravel information. The Fortune II experiment’s first case took about 192 seconds; the batch timed out at 300.
Bush-only, glowstone and melon reversal
Attempts timed out at 360, 120 and 120 seconds respectively. Short loot-pattern probability rules did not establish a comparably useful shortcut on holdout data.
Spawner timing, blaze positions and barters
Interesting information sources, but hidden attempts, measurement and call schedules remain unresolved. Ordinary F3 does not directly show arbitrary entity XYZ.

Superseded pilots—including a wrong blaze enum ordinal and an omitted glowstone RNG call—are not counted as successes. A remote F3/pie portal-eye counter was not found.

THE BOUNDARY OF THE CLAIM

What this page does not establish.

  • No completed human trial. Nobody used the full observation protocol and then visited the portal as part of these tests. Real aiming, rendering and acquisition time are still unknown.
  • No calibrated live-match probabilities. Ranked’s private seed filtering and route choice can change the odds. Uniform-seed statistics are reference estimates.
  • No version-independent promise. Standardized eyes, default first-ring placement where relevant, and all per-method conditions matter. Vanilla filling, disabled standardization or changed versions are different cases.
  • No competition approval. Manual entry does not make an unapproved calculator permissible. The motion route internally recovers full 64-bit RNG seeds; if that is the world seed, it implicates the explicit full-world-seed prohibition. Hiding the seed from output does not avoid that issue.

This page explains private/offline research. It does not distribute a seed cracker, proprietary JAR, decompiled game code or game-state extraction tool.
